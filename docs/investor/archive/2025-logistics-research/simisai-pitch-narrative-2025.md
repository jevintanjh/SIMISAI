## ACT 1: THE CRISIS (2-3 minutes)

### Scene 1: The Multilingual Tower of Babel

**Visual**: Photo of warehouse floor with workers from different countries

**Narrative**:
"Walk into any Singapore warehouse today. You'll hear Indonesian, Mandarin, Thai, Filipino, Vietnamese, Malay. 60% of logistics workers are foreign nationals. Why? Because there's a 15-20% labor shortage. Singaporeans don't want these jobs. So companies hire from across Southeast Asia.

But here's the problem: Only 30-40% speak conversational English. Safety training? Lost in translation. Equipment instructions? Misunderstood. The result? 60% of workplace incidents are linked to communication gaps. 45% of equipment errors come from instruction misunderstanding.

And it costs S$2,500 to S$4,000 to train each worker with translated materials and interpreters. With 35-45% annual turnover, companies are spending millions just to keep workers from hurting themselves or breaking equipment."

**Key Stats on Screen**:
- 60% foreign workers
- 5+ languages spoken
- S$2,500-4,000 training cost per worker
- 35-45% annual turnover
- 60% of accidents from communication gaps

### Scene 2: The E-Commerce Tsunami

**Visual**: Chart showing exponential parcel volume growth

**Narrative**:
"Now add fuel to this fire: E-commerce is exploding. Singapore processed 450 million parcels in 2024. This year? 650 million. That's 40% growth in ONE year.

During Singles Day, 11.11, Black Friday - peak season hits 300-400% normal volume. Warehouses designed for 100,000 parcels a day suddenly need to handle 300,000. And customers expect same-day delivery.

What happens? Manual sorting accuracy - normally 95% - plummets to 85%. That means 15 out of every 100 parcels goes to the wrong place. At S$8-12 to fix each error, the industry is burning S$150-300 million annually just on sorting mistakes.

And returns? 15-25% of e-commerce orders come back. That's 80-100 million parcels going in reverse. Each return costs 2-3x forward delivery. The economics are breaking."

**Key Stats on Screen**:
- 450M → 650M parcels (+40% YoY)
- 300-400% peak season volume surges
- 85% sorting accuracy during peaks (vs. 95% baseline)
- S$150-300M annual error cost industry-wide
- 15-25% return rate (2-3x reverse logistics cost)

### Scene 3: The Margin Death Spiral

**Visual**: Shrinking profit margin chart over time

**Narrative**:
"So how much profit are these companies making to absorb all these costs? 3-5%. Three to five percent net margins.

Let me break down the math: Each parcel costs S$4.50-7.00 to process and deliver. Companies charge S$4.80-7.50. That's maybe 50 cents profit per parcel. IF nothing goes wrong.

But everything IS going wrong:
- Wages up 12-15% per year
- Rent up 8-12% per year
- Foreign worker levies up 18% in 2024 alone
- Customer expectations: same-day delivery, free returns, real-time tracking

Meanwhile, Amazon and Alibaba's logistics arms are offering 20-30% lower prices to win market share. They can afford to lose money on logistics because they make it up on e-commerce platforms.

Local 3PLs can't compete. They're hemorrhaging customers. 10-15% volume decline in 2024. At 3% margins, that's the difference between survival and bankruptcy."

**Key Stats on Screen**:
- 3-5% net margins (down from 8-10% in 2020)
- S$4.50-7.00 cost per parcel
- S$4.80-7.50 revenue per parcel
- Wage inflation: +12-15% YoY
- Rent inflation: +8-12% YoY
- Levy increase: +18% (2024)
- Volume decline: -10-15% (2024) due to Amazon/Alibaba

### Scene 4: The Automation Wall

**Visual**: Giant locked door labeled "S$2.5 MILLION"

**Narrative**:
"Okay, so the solution is obvious: Automate. Robots don't need translators. They don't call in sick. They work 24/7. Problem solved.

Except... traditional warehouse automation costs S$2.5 to S$4.2 million. For a MEDIUM-SIZED warehouse. And it takes 5-7 years to pay back.

Let me say that again: If you're a logistics company making 3-5% margins, you need to invest 2-3 years of TOTAL REVENUE to automate. And wait 5-7 years to break even. Most companies don't have that kind of cash or patience.

The result? Only 15-20% of Singapore warehouses have automation. The other 80-85%? They're stuck manually sorting parcels, hiring workers they can't train fast enough, and watching Amazon steal their customers.

80% of Singapore logistics companies are SMEs. 90% of them CAN'T AFFORD to automate. They're locked out."

**Key Stats on Screen**:
- Traditional automation: S$2.5-4.2M
- ROI payback: 5-7 years
- Only 15-20% of warehouses automated
- 80% of logistics companies are SMEs
- 90% can't afford traditional automation

## ACT 3: THE SIMISAI SOLUTION (2-3 minutes)

### Scene 6: Computer Vision + Multilingual AI

**Visual**: Split screen - warehouse worker with AR glasses on left, SIMISAI interface on right

**Narrative**:
"This is where SIMISAI comes in. We asked a simple question: What if you didn't need S$2.5 million worth of conveyors and robots? What if you could make your EXISTING workers 50% more productive, using technology that costs 90% less?

Here's how it works:

**Computer Vision**: We use cameras and AI to detect packages in real-time. The system sees what the worker sees. It reads barcodes, checks sorting destinations, and verifies placement. If a worker puts a parcel in the wrong bin, it alerts them INSTANTLY. No more misrouted packages.

**Multilingual AI Chat**: Every worker gets real-time guidance in their native language. Indonesian worker doesn't understand English equipment manual? Ask SIMISAI in Indonesian. Get step-by-step instructions. With pictures. With videos. In the language they actually speak.

The result? Workers who used to take 3 months to get up to speed are productive in 2 weeks. Sorting errors drop 60-70%. Training costs cut in half. And it costs S$50,000 to S$150,000 to implement. Not S$2.5 million.

ROI? 6-12 months. Not 5-7 years."

**Key Stats on Screen**:
- SIMISAI: S$50K-150K (vs. S$2.5-4.2M traditional)
- ROI: 6-12 months (vs. 5-7 years)
- 90% lower upfront cost
- 10x faster payback
- Real-time CV verification + multilingual guidance

## APPENDIX: HANDLING OBJECTIONS

### Objection 1: "Why won't customers just wait for traditional automation to get cheaper?"

**Response**:
"Traditional automation won't get cheaper fast enough. The cost is mostly hardware - conveyors, sorters, robots. That's physical infrastructure with material costs that don't scale like software.

Plus, customers don't have TIME to wait. Amazon is stealing their business NOW. Foreign worker levies are increasing NOW. Peak season 2025 is in 6 months.

SIMISAI is software-first. We use existing warehouse infrastructure + cameras. Our cost drops with scale (software leverage), while traditional automation stays hardware-bound.

And frankly, traditional automation vendors don't WANT to go downmarket. They make more money selling S$10M systems to Fortune 500 companies. They're not interested in S$50K-150K SME deals. That's OUR market."

### Objection 2: "What about AI translation tools like Google Translate? Can't workers just use that?"

**Response**:
"Generic translation misses logistics-specific terminology. 'LTL shipment', 'cross-dock', 'pick-and-pack', 'sorter chute' - these don't translate well in Google Translate. You get gibberish.

Plus, workers don't have time to pull out phones, type questions, read translations during sorting. They need REAL-TIME guidance. Voice-based. Hands-free. With visual verification.

SIMISAI is trained on logistics terminology in 5 languages. We know that 'batch picking' in Indonesian is 'pengambilan batch', but more importantly, we know what actions that entails in a Singapore warehouse context. Context matters.

And the computer vision integration is key. We're not just translating words. We're verifying actions. Did the worker actually put the parcel in the right bin? Generic translation can't do that."

### Objection 3: "Won't customers worry about job displacement? Worker pushback?"

**Response**:
"This is AI-ASSISTED, not AI-REPLACED. We're not firing workers. We're making them more productive.

In fact, customers LOVE that messaging. It aligns with Singapore government priorities. The goal isn't to eliminate jobs. It's to reduce dependence on constantly hiring MORE foreign workers, which is government-restricted anyway.

Workers love it too. Instead of feeling stupid because they can't understand English instructions, they get guidance in their language. It empowers them. Reduces stress. Improves safety. Makes jobs BETTER, not obsolete.

We've tested this in pilots. Worker satisfaction went UP. They feel supported, not replaced. That's huge for retention in an industry with 35-45% turnover."

### Objection 4: "What's your moat if Honeywell or Siemens decides to add multilingual features?"

**Response**:
"Three moats:

**Moat 1: Data**. We're collecting millions of multilingual interactions from the 60% foreign worker demographic that Honeywell ignores. In 2 years, we'll have the largest logistics-specific multilingual dataset in ASEAN. Honeywell would be starting from zero.

**Moat 2: Focus**. Honeywell targets Fortune 500. Their sales cycle is 12-18 months. Their deals are S$5-10M. They're not set up to sell S$50K-150K deals to SMEs. Different GTM motion, different customer profile. We're not competing for the same customers.

**Moat 3: Government relationships**. We're a Singapore company. We're aligned with IMDA, Enterprise SG, AI Singapore. We help customers get grants. Honeywell is a multinational. They don't navigate Singapore SME grants. That's a distribution moat.

By the time Honeywell realizes ASEAN SME logistics is a massive market, we'll have 50-100 customers, 5 years of multilingual data, and PSG/EDG pre-approvals. That's a 2-3 year head start minimum."

### Objection 5: "Margins are thin in logistics. Will customers pay for software?"

**Response**:
"That's EXACTLY why they'll pay. Thin margins mean they can't afford errors. Every misrouted parcel costs S$8-12. If we reduce errors by 60-70%, we're literally saving them S$60,000-100,000 per month for a mid-sized warehouse.

At S$80,000 one-time cost + S$3,000/month, we pay for ourselves in 8-10 months. That's not a cost. That's a profit center.

Plus, government grants cover 50-70% of the upfront cost. Customer is paying S$25,000-40,000 out of pocket for S$80,000 solution. Risk is tiny compared to payoff.

Thin margins also mean customers are DESPERATE for anything that improves efficiency. They're not looking for 10% improvement. They need 30-50% improvement to survive. That's what we deliver."
